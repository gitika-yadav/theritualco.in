Barre for Beginners: Terms, Form and Your First Two Weeks

By Gitika Yadav, founder of The Ritual Co · 28 November 2026 · 7 min read

Barre is one of the most beginner-friendly workouts there is. It is low impact, needs no dance background and can be done in a few square feet of your living room. The only thing that trips people up at first is the vocabulary.

This guide covers the words you will hear, how to hold good form, the mistakes almost everyone makes, and a simple two-week plan to get started.

Barre words, explained

  • Plié (plee-ay): a bend of the knees, with the knees tracking over the toes.
  • Relevé (rel-uh-vay): rising onto the balls of the feet.
  • First position: heels together, toes turned out to a comfortable V.
  • Second position: feet wide apart, toes turned out.
  • Parallel: feet hip-width apart, toes pointing forward.
  • Turnout: rotating the legs outward from the hips, not from the knees or feet.
  • Pulse: a tiny movement of about an inch, up and down, repeated quickly.
  • Hold: staying still at the hardest point of a move.
  • Tuck: a small curl of the pelvis under, using the lower abs and glutes. Modern classes keep this gentle rather than forced.
  • Chair or barre: whatever you hold for balance.

Good form in five cues

  1. Stand tall. Crown of the head up, shoulders down and away from your ears.
  2. Knees over toes. In every plié, your knees should point the same way as your toes. Turn out only as far as your hips allow.
  3. Light hand. Fingertips rest on the chair. If you are gripping, make the move smaller.
  4. Small range. Barre moves are meant to be tiny. Bigger is not better.
  5. Keep breathing. It is easy to hold your breath during holds and pulses. Exhale on the effort.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Forcing turnout. Twisting the feet out further than the hips rotate puts strain on the knees. Less turnout, done properly, is better.
  • Swinging the legs. Momentum makes leg lifts easier and less effective. Slow down and lift lower.
  • Leaning on the chair. This takes the work out of your legs and core.
  • Going too heavy. Start with no weight, then add light ankle or wrist weights once the moves feel controlled.
  • Pushing through sharp pain. Shaking muscles are normal. Pinching or sharp pain in the knees, hips or back is not: stop or make the move smaller.

What you need to start

A chair, a mat and, on smooth floors, a pair of grip socks. Once you are comfortable, a small 25cm ball and light ankle weights make the same routine much harder. The full list is in our barre equipment guide.

Your first two weeks

Three short sessions a week is plenty to begin with. Leave a day between sessions while your legs adapt.

Week 1 · 3 × 15 minutes, no weights
  • Warm-up: 2 minutes of relevés and gentle hip circles
  • Pliés in first position: 3 sets of 30 seconds, holding the last 10 seconds
  • Second-position squat: 3 sets of 30 seconds
  • Back leg lifts: 45 seconds per side
  • Side leg lifts: 45 seconds per side
  • Bridge on the mat: 1 minute
  • Stretch: 2 minutes
Week 2 · 3 × 20 minutes, add pulses and the ball

Follow our 20-minute barre routine at home. Add pulses at the end of each set, and squeeze a small ball between your thighs during pliés and bridges.

From week three, add light ankle weights to the leg lifts or try our barre arm workout on a fourth day.

How soon will I notice a difference?

Most people notice better balance and steadier legs within a few weeks of regular practice. Visible changes depend on consistency, sleep, food and everything else you do in the week, so focus on turning up two or three times a week rather than on any single session.

If you are pregnant, recovering from an injury or have a health condition, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before starting a new workout.

Frequently asked questions

Is barre good for beginners?

Yes. Barre is low impact, needs no dance experience and every move can be made smaller or easier. Holding a chair for balance makes it accessible even if your balance is not great yet.

How often should a beginner do barre?

Two or three sessions of 15–20 minutes a week is a good start. Leave a rest day between sessions in the first couple of weeks while your legs adapt.

Do I need dance experience for barre?

No. Barre borrows a few ballet positions such as plié and relevé, but the movements are simple and explained in every class or routine.

Why do my legs shake during barre?

Shaking is normal and means the muscles are working close to their limit during long holds and pulses. Sharp pain or pinching is different: stop or make the movement smaller.

What should I wear for barre at home?

Comfortable fitted clothes you can move in, and grip socks or bare feet. Grip socks help on smooth floors.
