Barre Equipment for Home: What You Actually Need

By Gitika Yadav, founder of The Ritual Co · 28 November 2026 · 5 min read

You do not need a ballet barre, a mirror wall or a studio membership to do barre properly at home. You need something steady to hold, a surface you will not slip on, and two or three small props that make the movements harder.

Here is the full list, in order of importance, with what each piece actually does.

1. Something to hold: the "barre"

In a studio, the barre is a rail at about hip height. At home, any of these work:

  • The back of a sturdy dining chair that will not tip
  • A kitchen counter or a stable table edge
  • A windowsill or the top of a low bookcase

You only rest your fingertips on it for balance. If you find yourself leaning your weight into it, the move is too big or your leg is too high.

A wall-mounted or freestanding ballet barre is nice to have if you train often, but it is the last thing to buy, not the first.

2. Grip socks

Most Indian homes have tiled, marble or wooden floors, which get slippery the moment you rise onto your toes or turn out into a plié. Grip socks have small silicone dots on the sole that hold the floor, so you can work in bare-foot positions without sliding. They are also more hygienic than bare feet on a shared mat.

3. A small soft ball

The 25cm soft ball is the signature barre prop. Squeezing it between your inner thighs during pliés, bridges and curl-ups switches on muscles that are hard to target otherwise. It also goes behind your knee for hamstring curls and under your back for core work on the mat.

The Ritual Ball is the standard 25cm size, anti-burst and soft enough to squeeze.

4. Ankle and wrist weights

Barre uses very light weight, because the sets are long. Ankle weights make leg lifts, kickbacks and side lifts much harder without changing the movement. Worn on the wrists, the same weights turn arm circles and presses into a real burn.

The Ritual Cuffs are soft silicone, 2 lb (about 0.9 kg) each, adjustable for wrists or ankles, and sold as a pair. See our barre exercises with ankle weights.

5. Light dumbbells (optional)

Many barre classes include an arm section with 0.5–1kg hand weights. If you already wear cuffs on your wrists you can skip these, but small dumbbells make it easier to do presses and curls. Our capsule dumbbells come in 1kg and 2kg. Start with 1kg for barre arms.

6. A mat

Most barre sessions end with core and glute work on the floor. A mat with grip and some cushioning protects your knees and spine. The Ritual Mat is 5mm thick and 183 × 61 cm.

Optional extras

  • A Pilates ring adds inner-thigh and arm resistance in seated and standing work. The Ritual Ring.
  • A resistance band for glute work, if you already have one.

What to skip

  • Heavy weights. Barre is about high repetitions; anything over 1–2kg per hand or ankle usually ruins your form.
  • A mirror wall. Nice, not necessary. A phone propped up to film a set works just as well for checking form.
  • Special shoes. Barre is done in socks or bare feet.

The minimal barre kit

If you buy only three things: grip socks, a 25cm ball and a pair of ankle weights. With a chair and a mat you already own, that covers almost every move in a home barre class. Try it with our 20-minute barre routine.

If you are pregnant, recovering from an injury or have a health condition, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before starting a new workout.

Frequently asked questions

What equipment do I need for barre at home?

A sturdy chair or counter to hold, grip socks, a 25cm soft ball, light ankle or wrist weights and a mat. The chair replaces a ballet barre; everything else is small and easy to store.

Do I need a ballet barre for barre workouts?

No. The back of a sturdy chair, a kitchen counter or a windowsill at about hip height works. You only rest your hand on it lightly for balance.

What size ball is used in barre?

Most barre classes use a soft ball of about 25cm. It is small enough to squeeze between the thighs or knees and soft enough to compress.

How heavy should weights be for barre?

Light. Around 0.5–1kg hand weights or 2 lb (about 0.9 kg) ankle and wrist weights are typical, because barre uses long sets of small movements.

Do I need grip socks for barre?

On smooth tiles, marble or wooden floors, yes. Grip socks stop your feet sliding in pliés and relevés and help you balance.
