Barre Exercises with Ankle Weights: 8 Moves for Legs and Glutes

By Gitika Yadav, founder of The Ritual Co · 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

Ankle weights are the simplest way to make barre harder. The movements stay exactly the same, but every leg lift, kickback and pulse has to move a little extra load, which you will feel within the first minute.

Below are eight moves for legs and glutes, five at the chair and three on the mat, that take about 15 minutes in total.

How heavy should ankle weights be for barre?

Light. Barre sets are long and the range of movement is small, so around 0.5–1kg per ankle is the usual range. The Ritual Cuffs are 2 lb, about 0.9 kg each, sold as a pair, with an adjustable strap that also fits wrists. If you are new to barre, do the routine without weights for a couple of weeks first.

Before you start

  • Strap the cuffs just above the ankle bone, snug but not tight.
  • Wear grip socks on smooth floors so the standing foot stays put.
  • Warm up for two minutes with relevés and hip circles, without the weights.
  • Keep every lift slow and small. If your torso starts swinging, lower the leg.

The 8 moves

At the chair · 8 min

1. Back leg pulses1 min per side

Face the chair, hinge forward slightly from the hips with a long spine and extend one leg behind you, toe touching the floor. Lift the leg a few inches and pulse up and down without arching your lower back.

Keep both hips facing the chair; the lift is small.

2. Side leg lifts1 min per side

Stand side-on to the chair, standing knee soft. Lift the outside leg out to the side with the toe pointing forward, to hip height or lower, then lower with control.

Last 15 seconds: hold at the top and pulse.

3. Attitude lifts45 sec per side

Face the chair. Bend the working knee and turn it slightly out, then lift the knee and foot together behind you, as if pressing the heel towards the ceiling.

4. Standing hamstring curls45 sec per side

Face the chair, weight on one leg. Bend the other knee to bring the heel towards your seat, then lower slowly. Keep your knees side by side.

5. Front leg lifts45 sec per side

Stand tall side-on to the chair. Lift a straight leg in front of you a few inches off the floor, then lower without touching down.

Works the front of the hip and the core; keep the torso still.

On the mat · 6 min

6. Kneeling kickbacks1 min per side

On the mat on hands and knees. Extend one leg straight back at hip height and pulse it up an inch, keeping the hips level.

7. Fire hydrants45 sec per side

Still on hands and knees, lift one bent knee out to the side, keeping the knee bent at 90 degrees, then lower.

8. Side-lying clamshells and lifts1 min per side

Lie on your side, knees bent. Open the top knee like a clamshell for 30 seconds, then straighten the top leg and lift and lower it for 30 seconds.

Making it easier or harder

  • Easier: take the weights off, reduce each set to 30 seconds, or lift the leg lower.
  • Harder: add a 10-second hold at the top of each move, or squeeze a small ball behind the knee during hamstring curls.

Using ankle weights safely

  • Use them for slow, controlled movement, not for running, jumping or fast kicks.
  • Stop if you feel strain in the knee or lower back rather than the muscle you are working.
  • Take them off for the stretch at the end.

Frequently asked questions

Are ankle weights good for barre?

Yes. Light ankle weights make barre leg lifts, kickbacks and pulses harder without changing the movement, which suits barre's small, high-repetition style.

How heavy should ankle weights be for barre?

Around 0.5–1kg per ankle is the usual range for barre. The Ritual Cuffs are 2 lb, about 0.9 kg each.

Can beginners use ankle weights in barre?

It is best to learn the moves without weights for a couple of weeks first. Once you can keep the movement slow and controlled, add light ankle weights.

Can I wear ankle weights on my wrists?

If the strap is adjustable, yes. The Ritual Cuffs fit both wrists and ankles, so the same pair works for barre arm sections too.
