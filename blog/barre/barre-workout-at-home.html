What Is a Barre Workout? A 20-Minute Barre Routine at Home

By Gitika Yadav, founder of The Ritual Co · 22 November 2026 · 7 min read

Barre has quietly become one of the most-booked classes in studios, and it is easy to see why. It is low impact, it leaves your legs shaking in the best way, and it never feels like a punishing gym session. The good news: you do not need a studio, or even a barre, to do it.

This guide explains what barre actually is, how it differs from Pilates, and walks you through a 20-minute routine you can do at home with a chair.

What is a barre workout?

Barre borrows the positions of ballet — pliés, relevés, turned-out stances and leg lifts — and combines them with Pilates-style core work. Most of the class is done standing, holding a barre lightly for balance, using small, controlled movements repeated many times. Those tiny "pulses" of an inch up and an inch down are what make barre so effective for the legs, glutes and core without jumping or heavy weights.

You do not need any dance experience. If you can stand tall and hold the back of a chair, you can do barre.

Barre vs Pilates

Barre

Mostly standing at a barre or chair. Ballet positions, small pulses and holds. Builds endurance in the legs and seat, with core and arms worked too.

Pilates

Mostly on the mat. Controlled, full-range movement and spinal work. Builds deep core control and posture.

They pair beautifully and use the same props — a small ball, light weights and grip socks. Many people do Pilates on two days and barre on one or two others. New to both? Our Pilates for beginners guide is a good companion read.

What you need

  • A "barre"The back of a sturdy chair, a kitchen counter or a windowsill at about hip height. You only rest your hand on it.
  • Grip socksOn smooth tiles or wooden floors, grip socks stop your feet sliding in pliés and relevés.
  • Light weights1–2 lb per side is plenty. The Ritual Cuffs are 2 lb per cuff and go on wrists or ankles.
  • A small ballSqueezed between the knees or thighs to switch on the inner thighs. The Ritual Ball is the standard 25cm size.

You will also want a mat for the short core section at the end.

The 20-minute barre routine

Do each move for the time shown, moving slowly and keeping your hand light on the chair. Breathe throughout. If anything pinches, make the movement smaller or skip it.

Warm-up · 3 min

1. Rise and roll1 min

Stand facing the chair, feet hip-width. Rise onto the balls of your feet and lower slowly. Then roll down through your spine towards the floor and roll back up.

2. Side reach and hip circles2 min

Reach one arm overhead and lean gently to the side, then switch. Finish with slow hip circles in both directions.

Legs & seat at the chair · 10 min

3. Plié pulses2 min

Heels together, toes turned out to a comfortable V. Bend your knees out over your toes and lower a few inches, then pulse an inch up and down. For the last 30 seconds, rise onto your toes and keep pulsing.

Add the ball: hold it between your inner thighs just above the knees and squeeze as you pulse.

4. Wide second-position squat2 min

Step your feet wide, toes turned out. Lower into a wide squat with your knees tracking over your toes. Pulse at the bottom, then hold for 15 seconds.

5. Back leg lifts1 min per side

Face the chair, hinge forward slightly and extend one leg behind you, toe on the floor. Lift the leg a few inches and pulse, keeping your hips square.

Add resistance: wear a cuff on each ankle.

6. Side leg lifts1 min per side

Stand side-on to the chair. Lift the outside leg out to the side, to hip height or lower, toe pointing forward, and pulse. Keep your torso tall rather than leaning over.

7. Relevé calf raises2 min

Feet parallel, rise high onto the balls of your feet and lower slowly. For the last 30 seconds, hold at the top and pulse.

Arms · 3 min

8. Arm circles and presses3 min

Stand tall in a small plié, arms out to the sides at shoulder height. Make small forward circles for 45 seconds, then backward circles, then press your arms back and forth, then pulse them overhead.

Add resistance: wear the cuffs on your wrists — light weight goes a long way here.

Core on the mat · 3 min

9. Ball curl-ups1.5 min

Lie on your back, knees bent, ball between your knees. Squeeze the ball and curl your head and shoulders up, then lower slowly.

10. Bridge pulses1.5 min

Same position, ball still between your knees. Lift your hips into a bridge and pulse an inch up and down, squeezing the ball.

Stretch · 1 min

Hug your knees to your chest, then do a gentle figure-four stretch for each hip.

Tips for barre at home

  • Small means hard. The movements should be tiny. If you are swinging your leg high, you are using momentum instead of muscle.
  • Light hand on the chair. It is for balance only. If you are leaning on it, lower your leg.
  • Feel the shake. Trembling legs are normal in barre — it means the muscle is working.
  • Consistency over intensity. Two or three 20-minute sessions a week will do more than one exhausting hour.

If you are pregnant, recovering from an injury or have a health condition, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before starting a new workout.

Frequently asked questions

What is a barre workout?

Barre is a low-impact workout that borrows positions from ballet — pliés, relevés and leg lifts — and mixes them with Pilates-style core work and small, high-repetition movements. You hold a barre, or at home a sturdy chair or counter, for balance.

Can I do barre at home without a barre?

Yes. The back of a sturdy chair, a kitchen counter or a windowsill at roughly hip height works as a barre. You only need it for light balance, not to lean your weight on.

What is the difference between barre and Pilates?

Pilates is mostly done on a mat and centres on controlled core work and spinal movement. Barre is mostly standing, uses ballet positions and small pulsing movements for the legs and seat, and adds core and arm work. Many people do both, and they use similar props.

What equipment do I need for barre at home?

Nothing is essential beyond a chair and a non-slip surface. Grip socks help you stay steady on smooth floors, light ankle or wrist weights add resistance to leg lifts and arm work, and a small 25cm ball is used between the knees or thighs in many barre moves.

How often should I do barre?

Two to four sessions a week is a good rhythm for most people. Because it is low impact, barre is easy to fit around walking, yoga or strength training.
