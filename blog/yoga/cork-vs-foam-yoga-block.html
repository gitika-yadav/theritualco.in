Cork vs Foam Yoga Block: Which Is Best for You?

Walk into any design-led yoga studio in India and you'll see both kinds of blocks in the corner — chunky natural cork and lighter EVA foam in soft pastels. Both do the same job: they bring the floor closer to your hands, deepen stretches and add support in balance poses.

The real question isn't "which is better" — it's which fits your practice, your hands and your budget. Here's the honest comparison.

Cork blocks

Pros:

  • Excellent grip — cork gets tackier as your hands sweat, so it seldom slips
  • Very stable — heavier, so it doesn't shift between poses
  • Natural, sustainable material that ages nicely

Cons:

  • Heavy and harder to carry to class
  • Absorbs sweat, oil and dye — stains over time and needs care
  • Hard surface under the wrists and hands
  • Significantly more expensive (₹1,000+)

Foam (EVA) blocks

Pros:

  • Lightweight and easy to carry
  • Wipe-clean and hygienic — sweat doesn't soak in
  • Gentle on wrists and hands, thanks to slight give
  • Affordable — a good dense block costs ₹900 or less
  • Available in the same soft, aesthetic colourways

Cons:

  • Light — may slide if it's a flimsy, low-density block (density matters)
  • Can dent or compress over years of hard use

The verdict

If you sweat heavily, want a natural-material practice and don't mind the weight and price, cork is lovely. But for everyday yoga and pilates support work, a dense EVA foam block is the better all-rounder: stable enough for proper support, light enough to carry, and wipe-clean in a household that shares equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a cork or foam yoga block better?

Cork blocks offer better grip, more stability and a natural surface, but they absorb sweat, are heavier and cost more. Dense EVA foam blocks are light, wipe-clean, joint-friendly and affordable — the better choice for everyday practice.

Are foam yoga blocks good enough?

Yes. High-density EVA foam blocks are stable enough for seated poses, triangles and balance work, and they're kinder to your hands than hard cork. Material matters less than density and size.

What size yoga block should I buy?

The standard block is roughly 23cm x 15cm x 10cm, which suits most practitioners for both the low and high placements.

Can I use a yoga block for pilates?

Yes. A block adds support to stretching, seated poses and balance moves in pilates, exactly as it does in yoga.
