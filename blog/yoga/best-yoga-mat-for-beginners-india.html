Best Yoga Mat for Beginners in India: How to Choose

By Gitika Yadav, founder of The Ritual Co · 16 November 2026

The best yoga mat for a beginner is one that grips, lies flat and is 4–6 mm thick. Grip comes first. On a slippery mat your hands slide in downward dog and your feet slide in warrior, and you spend the class fighting the mat instead of learning the poses.

Everything else, including colour, brand and even price, matters less than those three things. This guide explains each one, compares the common mat materials sold in India, and helps you decide how much to spend on your first mat.

What a beginner yoga mat needs

1. Grip

You need grip in two places: between your skin and the mat, and between the mat and the floor. Check both. A mat with a sticky top that skates across a tiled floor is no better than a slippery one. Natural rubber bases hold tiled and wooden floors well, which matters in most Indian homes.

2. Thickness: 4–6 mm

Thin mats (1–3 mm) are made for travel and are hard on knees and wrists. Very thick mats (8 mm and above) feel comfortable when you kneel but make standing balances wobbly, because your foot sinks in. For a first mat, 4–6 mm gives you cushioning and stability together.

3. A mat that lies flat

Light mats tend to curl at the ends and bunch up under you when you step forward. A denser, heavier mat unrolls flat and stays where you put it. The trade-off is weight: a dense mat is less convenient to carry to a class.

4. Size

The standard size is about 183 cm × 61 cm, which suits most people. If you are taller than about 180 cm, look for a longer mat.

Prices are typical ranges on Indian marketplaces and brand sites, and they vary by brand and size.

PVC is the mat most people start on because it is cheap and sold everywhere. Its weakness is grip once your palms get warm. TPE is a lighter foam with a little more grip, and it is easy to carry. Cork grips better as it gets damp, so it suits people who sweat, though it feels firm. Natural rubber with a PU top gives the strongest grip and the most stable base. It is heavier, costs more, and is not suitable if you have a latex allergy.

How much should you spend on a first mat?

It depends on how sure you are that you will keep practising.

  • Just trying yoga for a few weeks: a basic PVC or TPE mat is enough. Put a towel over it if your hands slip.
  • Practising two or more times a week: buy a grippy mat from the start. Good grip makes poses easier to learn, and a well-made mat lasts for years, so you avoid buying twice.

Looking after your mat

  • Wipe it with a damp cloth after practice and let it dry before rolling.
  • Keep it out of direct sunlight, which dries out rubber and fades colour.
  • Roll it with the top side facing out so the ends lie flat next time.
  • Avoid oils and harsh cleaners on PU and rubber surfaces.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best yoga mat for beginners?

A mat that grips, lies flat and is about 4–6 mm thick. Grip matters most: a beginner on a slippery mat spends the class fighting the mat instead of learning the poses. Natural rubber or PU-topped mats grip best; basic PVC and TPE mats are cheaper but more slippery.

How thick should a beginner yoga mat be?

4–6 mm suits most beginners. It cushions knees and wrists while staying firm enough for balance poses. Mats thicker than about 8 mm feel soft but make standing balances wobbly.

Which yoga mat material is best?

Natural rubber with a PU top layer gives the best grip and stays put on the floor. TPE is lighter and cheaper with moderate grip. PVC is the cheapest and most slippery when you sweat. Cork grips well when damp but feels firm.

How much should a beginner spend on a yoga mat in India?

Basic PVC and TPE mats cost roughly ₹500–1,500. Natural rubber and PU-topped mats usually cost ₹3,000–6,000 and last several years. If you are only trying yoga out, start cheap; if you plan to practise a few times a week, a grippy mat is worth it from the start.

Is a 4 mm or 6 mm yoga mat better for beginners?

Both work. Choose 4 mm if you want more stability for standing poses and a lighter mat to carry, and 5–6 mm if your knees or wrists are sensitive.

Can I use a yoga mat for Pilates?

Yes. A grippy 5–6 mm yoga mat works well for mat Pilates. Very thin travel mats are uncomfortable for rolling exercises on the spine.
