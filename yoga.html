What makes a good yoga mat?

Grip, weight and thickness. A mat that slides or curls at the corners pulls your attention out of the pose. The Ritual Mat has a dense natural rubber base (about 2.8 kg) that holds the floor and unrolls flat, with an open-cell PU top that absorbs sweat instead of turning slick. At 5 mm it cushions knees and wrists without feeling unstable in balance poses. It contains no PVC or TPE and is covered by a 1-year warranty.

Why use a yoga block and belt?

Props aren't a shortcut — they're how you practise with good alignment while your flexibility catches up. A yoga block raises the floor in poses like triangle and half-moon, and supports your hips or spine in restorative work. A yoga belt lets you hold a forward fold or reclined hamstring stretch with a long spine instead of straining to reach your feet. Yoga Kits & Equipment – FAQ

A good yoga kit covers three things: a mat that grips, a block for support and a belt for reach. The mat is the foundation, the block brings the floor closer in standing poses and supports you in restorative ones, and the belt helps you hold stretches you can't reach yet. The Ritual Co. Yoga Essentials Kit is exactly that: The Ritual Mat, The Ritual Block and The Ritual Belt.

For most people practising at home, 4–6 mm is the sweet spot: enough cushioning for knees and wrists, while staying firm enough for balance poses. The Ritual Mat is 5 mm thick with a natural rubber base and a sweat-absorbing PU top.

Natural rubber is heavier and denser, so it stays put on the floor, lies flat and grips well. PVC and TPE mats are lighter and cheaper but tend to slide, curl at the corners and turn slick with sweat. The Ritual Mat uses a natural rubber base and contains no PVC or TPE.

One block is enough to start. Two blocks are useful for supported bridge, restorative poses and keeping both hands lifted in standing poses, which is why the Yoga Studio Kit includes a belt and two blocks.

A yoga belt (also called a yoga strap) helps you hold stretches like seated forward folds and reclined hamstring stretches with a straight spine, instead of rounding forward to reach your feet. It is one of the most useful and least expensive yoga props.
